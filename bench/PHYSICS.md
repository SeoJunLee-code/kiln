# Rigid-body scenes at size

Generated by `node bench/physics.ts --write`. A walled floor, N crates and balls dropped on
it in four layers, and 4 movers pacing through the pile. 60 ticks to warm up, then the
median of 240 ticks (each column its own median). One thread, no rendering.

Node v24, Rapier 0.21.0 (deterministic build), one laptop-class machine. One run; expect some noise.

Measured at the machine's sustained speed, after six seconds of full load. A fixed loop of
integer work took 81 ms when the process started, 248 ms before the first row and 239 ms
after the last: where the first is much lower, a game's first seconds run that much faster
than these numbers, and where the last two differ, the rows are not comparable.

## Whole tick

"Solver" is the backend's own step. "Kiln" is everything else in the tick: keeping entities
and bodies in step (one read of every body, written back as fixed-point and compared the
next tick to see what a script changed), the movers' sweeps, and the fixed-point core.
"Written" is the same scene with a script writing to every body's position every tick: each
is then put in its new place, and a mover's sweep has to ask about the near ones one by one.
