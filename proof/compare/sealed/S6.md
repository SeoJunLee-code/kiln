# Brick breaker

A written spec for one small 2D game. Build it from this text alone. Nobody will answer
questions. Five sound files are in `game/sounds/`: `paddle.wav`, `brick.wav`, `lost.wav`,
`win.wav` and `music.wav`. Use these and make no others.

## The game

1. A paddle at the bottom moves left and right with the arrow keys and cannot leave the
   screen. A ball bounces off the left, right and top edges, off the paddle and off
   bricks. Where the ball meets the paddle decides its angle, as far as 60 degrees from
   straight up at the paddle's ends.
2. Bricks fill six rows of ten at the top. There are three kinds, drawn differently:
   plain bricks break at the first hit; hard bricks, the whole third row, break at the
   second hit and look cracked after the first; four steel bricks never break.
3. A broken brick scores 10. The score and the lives are shown at all times.
4. The ball starts resting on the paddle and moves with it; Space launches it.
5. A ball that passes the bottom edge costs one of three lives; the ball goes back on the
   paddle. With no lives left a panel shows `Game over` and the score.
6. When every brick that can break is broken a panel shows `You win` and the score.
   Enter on either panel starts the game again.
7. **Sound effects.** `paddle.wav` plays each time the ball leaves the paddle,
   `brick.wav` each time the ball hits a brick, `lost.wav` when a life is lost, and
   `win.wav` once on `You win`. Hitting a steel brick plays `brick.wav` lower in pitch,
   clearly unlike a brick that takes damage.
8. **A rising run.** Bricks broken one after another without the ball touching the paddle
   in between sound higher each time: the second break of such a run is higher than the
   first, and so on up to at least the sixth. Touching the paddle starts the run again at
   the ordinary pitch.
9. **Left and right.** A brick's sound comes more from the left speaker the further left
   the brick is, and more from the right the further right.
10. **Music.** `music.wav` loops without a gap from the moment the ball is first launched.
    It is quieter than the effects. While a panel is shown it stops; a new game starts it
    again from its beginning.
11. **Volume.** M switches all sound off and on. The minus and plus keys step the volume
    through 0 to 10; it starts at 7. The screen shows the volume as a number and shows
    `Muted` while sound is off. Muting does not pause the music: unmuted, it is where it
    would have been.
12. P pauses the game and the music together, and P again continues both from where they
    stopped.
13. No sound is cut off by another: two bricks hit close together are both heard.

## What "done" means

- Every file of the game is inside the folder `game/`.
- The game starts in the engine's ordinary way of running a game, with no error and no
  warning.
- Everything under "The game" holds for a person who plays with the arrow keys, Space, M,
  P, minus, plus and Enter, with stereo headphones on. Check as much of that as the engine
  lets you check without a person.

When done, list the files and say how the game is started.

## Screen pass checks

Checks 8 to 16 are judged by ear, with stereo headphones.

| # | Do | See or hear |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Start the game. | Six rows of ten bricks of three looks, a paddle with a ball on it, a score of 0, 3 lives, volume 7. Silence. No error or warning. |
| 2 | Move the paddle both ways. | The ball rides on it; the paddle stops at the edges. |
| 3 | Press Space. | The ball leaves; it bounces off the side and top edges. |
| 4 | Hit the ball with the paddle's middle and with each end. | Nearly straight up; steeply to that side. |
| 5 | Hit a plain brick, a third-row brick twice, a steel brick. | Gone, +10; cracked then gone, +10; unchanged, +0. |
| 6 | Miss the ball three times. | Lives 2, 1, then `Game over` with the score; each time before that the ball is back on the paddle. Enter starts again. |
| 7 | Break every breakable brick (or all but the four steel ones remain). | `You win` and the score. |
| 8 | Launch the ball. | Music starts at the launch, not before; quieter than the effects. |
| 9 | Listen through the end of the music file. | It goes round with no silence or click at the seam. |
| 10 | Paddle, plain brick, steel brick, lost life, win. | Five sounds that can be told apart; steel is the brick sound, lower. |
| 11 | Break three or more bricks before the ball comes back down. | Each break higher than the one before; after the paddle the next break is at the ordinary pitch. |
| 12 | Break a brick at the far left and one at the far right. | The first mostly in the left ear, the second mostly in the right. |
| 13 | Press M twice, thirty seconds apart, mid-music. | `Muted` and silence; then sound again, the music further on, not where it stopped. |
| 14 | Press minus to 0 and plus to 10. | The number follows; 0 is silent, 10 the loudest; no step beyond either. |
| 15 | Press P, wait, press P. | Game and music stop together and continue from the same place. |
| 16 | Reach a panel; press Enter and launch. | Music stops at the panel and starts from its beginning in the new game. Two bricks hit almost together are both heard. |

A game passes when all sixteen hold.
