# Life board

A written spec for one small program with a 2D board. Build it from this text alone.
Nobody will answer questions.

## The program

1. A board of 40 columns by 30 rows of square cells fills most of the screen. A cell is
   alive or dead, and the two are drawn in clearly different colours. Thin lines separate
   the cells.
2. The rule, applied to every cell at once: a live cell with two or three live neighbours
   stays alive; a dead cell with exactly three becomes alive; every other cell is dead in
   the next generation. Neighbours are the eight cells around. The board wraps: the
   left edge is next to the right, the top next to the bottom.
3. Clicking a cell with the left mouse button flips it. Holding the button and dragging
   paints every cell passed over to the state the first cell was flipped to.
4. A panel beside or below the board holds these controls, each a button with its name on
   it that reacts to a click and looks different while the pointer is over it:
   - `Play` starts the generations running; while they run the same button reads `Pause`
     and stops them.
   - `Step` advances one generation, only while paused.
   - `Clear` kills every cell, sets the generation to 0 and pauses.
   - `Random` fills the board with about a third of the cells alive. It gives the same
     board the first time after every start of the program.
   - `Slower` and `Faster` change the speed between 1, 2, 5, 10 and 30 generations per
     second. The panel shows the current speed. It starts at 5.
   - `Undo` takes back the last change made by hand or by `Clear`, `Random` or a stamp,
     up to twenty changes back. It does not take back generations.
5. Three pattern buttons, `Glider`, `Blinker` and `Block`. Clicking one selects it (the
   button stays marked) and the next click on the board stamps that pattern with its
   top-left at the clicked cell; the selection then ends. Pressing Escape drops a
   selection without stamping.
6. The panel shows, always up to date: `Generation N`, `Alive N` (live cells now), and
   the column and row under the pointer while it is over the board, as `Cell C,R`,
   counted from 0 at the top left.
7. Keys do the same as the buttons: Space is Play/Pause, N is Step, C is Clear, R is
   Random, the minus and plus keys are Slower and Faster, Z is Undo.
8. When a generation is the same as the one before it, the running stops by itself and
   the panel shows `Stable`. The word goes away at the next change.
9. The program opens paused on an empty board at generation 0.

## Screen pass checks

| # | Do | See |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Start it. | An empty 40 by 30 board with lines, a panel with the ten buttons, `Generation 0`, `Alive 0`, speed 5. No error or warning. |
| 2 | Move the pointer over the board and over a button. | `Cell C,R` follows it, 0,0 at the top left and 39,29 at the bottom right; the button looks different under the pointer. |
| 3 | Click a cell; click it again. | Alive, `Alive 1`; dead, `Alive 0`. |
| 4 | Drag across ten dead cells; drag back over them starting on a live one. | Ten alive; then dead again. |
| 5 | Click `Blinker`, click the board, click the board again. | The button is marked, three cells in a line appear with their first cell where clicked, the mark goes; the second click flips one cell only. |
| 6 | Click `Step` twice. | The line turns upright and back. `Generation 2`. |
| 7 | Clear, stamp `Block`, click `Play`. | Four cells; the running stops by itself with `Stable`. |
| 8 | Clear, stamp `Glider` near the bottom-right corner, `Play` at speed 30. | It moves diagonally, leaves by one edge and comes in on the opposite one. `Alive 5` throughout. |
| 9 | While it runs. | The play button reads `Pause`; `Step` does nothing; clicking `Pause` stops the count. |
| 10 | `Slower` to the lowest and `Faster` to the highest. | 1, 2, 5, 10, 30 and no further either way; the generations visibly run at that rate. |
| 11 | `Random`; note `Alive`; restart the program; `Random`. | About 400 alive (300 to 500); the same number and picture both times. |
| 12 | Flip three cells, `Clear`, then `Undo` four times. | The board before `Clear` comes back, then the three flips go one by one. |
| 13 | Select `Glider`, press Escape, click the board. | One cell flips; no glider. |
| 14 | Repeat checks 6, 7 and 12 with Space, N, C, R, minus, plus and Z only. | The same results. |
